# How to write each profile field

trendJack reads these three fields every day. The daily scan judges news against the pillars and the keywords. The drafter reads the voice guide word for word.

## Voice guide

The voice guide says how the company sounds: its tone, the vocabulary it reaches for, and the things it never says.

Write it so that someone drafting a post could match it. Name what you saw, not adjectives that would fit any company. The drafter reads it every time it writes a draft, so this field decides more than any other whether a draft sounds like the company.

Cover these points:

1. Tone. Describe it with an example from a page or a post. "Dry and specific: the pricing page says 'No seat fees. You pay for the rows you store.'" passes. "Friendly and professional" fails.
2. Vocabulary. List the words and phrases the company uses again and again. Note the terms of art it uses, and the ones it avoids.
3. Sentence shape. Note whether the company writes short or long sentences, in the first person plural or the third person, with or without questions.
4. What it never says. List the claims, words and registers that are absent from its pages, for example superlatives, emoji or hype.
5. Where the evidence is thin. If the site has little copy, or the company has no social presence, say so in one sentence.

Where the website and the social posts disagree on tone, describe the social voice. That is how the company actually talks.

## Content pillars

A content pillar is a recurring theme the company can credibly post about. Write 3 to 8 pillars. Three to six is usually right.

For each pillar:

- Give it a short title, 120 characters or fewer.
- Write a description of one or two sentences that says what belongs under it.

A pillar you cannot point to a page for does not belong. trendJack judges every signal against these pillars by name, so a vague pillar makes the daily scan vague.

A good pillar names a subject the company has a position on, for example "Warehouse automation for mid-size grocers". A bad pillar names a category that fits anyone, for example "Innovation" or "Thought leadership".

## Alert keywords

An alert keyword is a term that should make a news item relevant to the company. Cover these areas:

- the company's market
- its technologies
- its customers' industries
- the problems it sells against

Do not include the company's own name. trendJack is for finding news to comment on, not press mentions.

trendJack matches the keywords against the news every day. A keyword that is too generic floods the workspace. A keyword that is too narrow starves it. Prefer two-word or three-word terms to single generic words. trendJack keeps at most 40 keywords and drops duplicates without regard to case.
